Overview

Abstract

Underwater spatial reasoning depends on object boundaries and geometric cues that are obscured by scattering, color distortion, and low visibility. Existing underwater resources capture these cues through images, annotations, and depth maps, but provide fragmented supervision for vision-language models.

We present a spatial data engine that connects visual appearance with object-level geometry through enhancement, 2D and geometry-assisted question generation, and expert filtering. It produces 4.27M QA pairs across five capability groups. We use this corpus to train USI-Brain and introduce an Underwater Prior-guided Rectifier (UPR) that applies gated corrections to visual features.

USI-Bench evaluates the resulting capabilities with 6,525 held-out QA pairs. Across NautData, UWBench, and MarineEval, USI-Brain achieves the best or joint-best score on 28 of 32 metrics.

On the 15 USI-Bench tasks, USI-Brain improves over Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct by 5.76 mean points, rising to 7.63 points with UPR. Controlled ablations separate the source of these gains: geometry-assisted supervision improves depth and ordering, HUE-derived supervision improves all 15 tasks, and UPR contributes most strongly to global scene description. Both model variants are evaluated directly on raw underwater images.

What we contribute

Three pieces, one spatial system

The work treats underwater spatial intelligence as a connected data, model, and evaluation problem rather than an isolated visual-language task.

01

Spatial Data Engine

Connects enhanced imagery, instance annotations, object-linked depth, and reconstructed scenes into 4.27M quality-controlled QA pairs spanning five capability groups.

Approach

Method

The framework couples supervision construction with visual-feature adaptation. Geometry is used during data construction; inference only requires raw images and questions.

  1. 01Aggregate sources

    About 540K visual samples plus roughly 400 reconstructed Atlantis scenes.

  2. 02Enhance evidence

    HUE restores color, illumination, edges, and texture for supervision construction.

  3. 03Generate 2D QA

    Identity, attributes, locations, relations, visibility, and scene content.

  4. 04Lift into 3D

    Depth samples and reconstructed boxes support ordering, occlusion, distance, and viewpoint.

  5. 05Organize and review

    Five capability groups followed by expert selection, correction, and deduplication.

Supervision at scale

From fragmented resources to object-linked spatial evidence

The engine brings heterogeneous images, video frames, instance labels, depth, and reconstructed geometry into one QA representation while preserving the connection between an object, its image region, and its spatial cues.

Data foundation

A broad visual source pool

Approximately 540K visual samples are drawn from the ten datasets aggregated in NautData together with CoralVQA, WebUOT-1M, and about 400 reconstructed Atlantis scenes. The sources cover varied habitats, objects, visibility conditions, and annotation formats.

Candidate QA pairs are organized by capability and reviewed for answer evidence, localization consistency, and redundancy before entering the final corpus.

Model adaptation

One objective across 2D and 3D reasoning

USI-Brain is initialized from Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct. The vision encoder, multimodal merger, and language model are jointly fine-tuned on the combined 2D and geometry-assisted corpus for two epochs.

The model uses the standard QA cross-entropy objective without auxiliary distillation, allowing the component studies to attribute gains directly to supervision coverage and UPR.

HUE

Restore visual evidence

Degradation maps for color shift, scattering, and blur condition two paths: one corrects color and illumination, while the other restores local edges and texture. A learned pixel-wise map selects their balance at every location.

GeoQA

Lift objects into geometry

Object masks or boxes collect aligned depth samples. Camera intrinsics back-project them into 3D, where fitted object boxes provide supervision for depth, ordering, occlusion, distance, viewpoint, and stratification.

UPR

Rectify visual features

A DINOv2 prior encoder estimates scattering-related bias and absorption-recovery gain. A learned gate controls the correction, then raw and rectified visual sequences are interleaved for the language model.

Training supervision vs. inference

HUE, depth maps, fitted 3D boxes, and reconstructed scenes are used to build supervision offline. Both USI-Brain variants are evaluated on raw underwater images; no enhanced image or depth input is required at inference.

Training configuration

Full-stack adaptation at scale

Training uses mixed precision, gradient checkpointing, SDPA, and FSDP full sharding. The language model and vision encoder use separate learning rates, while the model, merger, and visual stack remain trainable.

Source-level isolation

Images, video frames, and all processed derivatives remain in the same split. Test sources from USI-Bench and all external benchmarks are excluded from training-QA construction.

Duplicate screening

Source IDs and QA links are checked alongside SHA-256 and perceptual hashes to reduce train-test leakage.

Full human audit

Fifteen independent annotators audited all 6,525 items. The initial 10% uncertain and 7% incorrect entries were revised before evaluation.

Task-aware scoring

Evaluation combines accuracy, Acc@IoU0.5, Recall@IoU0.5, Kendall's tau, macro-F1, and three independent semantic judges instead of reducing every capability to one generic metric.

What the experiments show

Gains follow the supervision that was added

The main results and controlled removals provide complementary evidence: the corpus improves broad task coverage, geometry targets spatial ordering, and feature rectification most strongly affects global scene understanding.

Corpus transfer

12 of 15 tasks improve without UPR

USI-Brain gains +17.0 in captioning, +15.7 in structural perception, and +10.1 in ordering over the Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct backbone.

Feature rectification

UPR changes the capability profile

USI-Brain + UPR improves 13 of 15 tasks and reaches the best or joint-best listed result on seven. Scene description rises from 47.3 to 65.3 under matched training data.

External transfer

The gains extend beyond USI-Bench

NautData VQA accuracy rises from 0.381 to 0.478, UWBench referring Acc@IoU0.7 improves by 13.87 points, and MarineEval spatial-relation assessment improves by 15 points.

Inference efficiency

A practical baseline and an explicit UPR trade-off

Single-image inference uses raw inputs and batch size 1 on an RTX 3090. UPR adds a prior encoder and doubles the post-merger visual sequence, improving scene understanding at a measurable latency and memory cost.

Current frontier

Distance estimation and environment recognition remain slightly below the Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct backbone, while foreground/background macro-F1 reaches 22.7. Detection is currently measured by Recall@IoU0.5, so precision-oriented analysis would better expose false positives. These tasks identify the clearest priorities for broader supervision and more complete spatial evaluation.
