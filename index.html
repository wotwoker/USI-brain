Overview

Abstract

Underwater spatial reasoning depends on object boundaries and geometric cues that are obscured by scattering, color distortion, and low visibility. Existing underwater resources capture these cues through images, annotations, and depth maps, but provide fragmented supervision for vision-language models.

We present a spatial data engine that connects visual appearance with object-level geometry through enhancement, 2D and geometry-assisted question generation, and expert filtering. It produces 4.27M QA pairs across five capability groups. We use this corpus to train USI-Brain and introduce an Underwater Prior-guided Rectifier (UPR) that applies gated corrections to visual features.

USI-Bench evaluates the resulting capabilities with 6,525 held-out QA pairs. Across NautData, UWBench, and MarineEval, USI-Brain achieves the best or joint-best score on 28 of 32 metrics.

Approach

Method

The framework couples supervision construction with visual-feature adaptation. Geometry is used during data construction; inference only requires raw images and questions.

HUE

Restore visual evidence

Hybrid Underwater Enhancement combines color correction and detail restoration with a learned pixel-wise fusion map.

GeoQA

Lift objects into geometry

Depth and reconstructed object boxes create supervision for ordering, occlusion, distance, viewpoint, and stratification.

UPR

Rectify visual features

Underwater priors predict bias and gain, while a learned gate controls the residual update applied to each visual token.
